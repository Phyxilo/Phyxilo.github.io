03 — Research & projects

From emulsion tracks to landmine detection

MSc thesis · METU · 2024

Study of proton interactions in the DsTau (NA65) experiment at the CERN-SPS

The first analysis of DsTau's 2018 run. I developed proton linking, a method that follows each beam proton from the front of the emulsion module through the whole detector, so primary interactions in each tungsten target can be selected cleanly. Efficiencies are parametrised as a function of track density and taken from data, and the reconstructed vertices are compared with Monte Carlo event generators in multiplicity, track angle and impact parameter.

Gamma Kalkani · 2026 — now

A portable dual-gamma backscatter detector for plastic landmines

Metal detectors miss minimum-metal and plastic anti-personnel mines. This detector sends collimated gammas from a 22Na source into the soil and reads the Compton backscatter with LYSO scintillators and SiPM arrays, picking up the density and Zeff contrast between explosive and soil. I led the full 3D Geant4 model (source, tungsten/lead collimator, scintillators and SiPM rings) and am now building the prototype around it: custom SiPM boards with time-over-threshold front-ends and an FPGA readout.

BOOST-NLO · 2026

Weight smoothing in NLO Monte Carlo with gradient-boosted trees

NLO generators with MC@NLO matching produce a fraction of negatively weighted events, which costs statistical power and CPU time. I treated the subtraction weights as a regression problem and trained XGBoost models with a custom asymmetric loss to smooth them for Drell-Yan, bb̄ and tt̄ production, benchmarked against Born spreading. A single model trained on mixed processes failed because of their very different cross-section scales, so the final pipeline routes each process to its own model.

BSc graduation project · METU · 2021

Study of Ds decays with machine learning techniques

A simulation study for DsTau before data-taking. I generated 400 GeV proton–nucleus interactions with Pythia for the Ds → τ signal and FLUKA for the hadronic background, computed kink angles and flight lengths from the particle momenta and vertices, and stored them as ROOT trees. Likelihood, Fisher and MLP classifiers in TMVA then separated signal from background using those two variables.
